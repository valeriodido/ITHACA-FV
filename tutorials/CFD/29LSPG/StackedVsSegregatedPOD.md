# Segregated vs. stacked POD in the turbulent LSPG ROM

This note compares the two trial spaces available in `ReducedLSPGUnsteadyNSTurb`, as implemented on the
`LSPG-stackedPOD` branch:

* **segregated** POD (default): every field $f\in\{U,p,k,\omega\}$ has its own POD basis and its own
  generalized coordinates;
* **stacked** (joint) POD (`jointPOD (U p);` in `system/ITHACAdict`): the fields of a group $\mathcal G$ share
  one basis computed from the stacked snapshots, hence one set of coordinates.

Every step is written as the code does it, from the snapshots to the online Levenberg–Marquardt (LM) solve.
Section 9 gives a (heuristic) explanation of why the two behave differently for the pressure, Section 10 works it out on a two-unknown model problem with a drag output, and
Section 12 maps the formulas to the source code.
The figures are generated by `python3 docFigures.py` (in this folder) and written to `images/doc/`.

## 0. Notation

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| $N_c$, $N_{\text{if}}$ | number of cells, number of internal faces |
| $V_c$, $\lvert\Omega\rvert=\sum_c V_c$ | cell volume, domain volume |
| $\mathbf M$ | diagonal mass matrix of cell volumes (each $V_c$ repeated per vector component) |
| $\langle \mathbf a,\mathbf b\rangle_{\mathbf M}=\mathbf a^\top\mathbf M\mathbf b$, $\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\mathbf M}$ | volume-weighted inner product / norm (discrete $L^2(\Omega)$) |
| $\mathcal F=\{U,p,k,\omega\}$ | cell fields; $\varphi$ is the face flux, always tied to $U$ |

| $\boldsymbol\mu$ | parameter (inlet velocity); runs $r=1,\dots,R$ with $\boldsymbol\mu_r$ |
| $m_r$, $m=\sum_r m_r$ | snapshots per run, total snapshots |
| $N_f$ | number of modes of field $f$ (`Nmodes*proj`), $N$ for the stacked group |

## 1. The full-order residual

The FOM is `pimpleFoam`-like with $k$–$\omega$ SST, second-order backward (BDF2) time stepping. With constant
$\Delta t$,
$$
\left.\frac{\partial \mathbf q}{\partial t}\right|^{n}\approx\frac{3\mathbf q^{n}-4\mathbf q^{n-1}+\mathbf q^{n-2}}{2\Delta t}.
$$

The ROM does not run PIMPLE: it assembles, at the state $\mathbf q^n$, the *residuals* of the segregated
equations, as volume-integrated `fvMatrix` residuals ($\mathbf A\mathbf x-\mathbf b$). With
$\nu_{\text{eff}}=\nu+\nu_t(k,\omega,U)$ (SST eddy viscosity recomputed from the current fields):

**Momentum**
$$
\mathbf R_U(\mathbf q^n)=\Big[\partial_t U+\nabla\!\cdot(\varphi\,U)-\nabla\!\cdot\!\big(\nu_{\text{eff}}\,\mathrm{dev2}(\nabla U)\big)\Big]_h^n+\big[\nabla p\big]_h^n ,
$$
where the convective flux $\varphi$ is a **state variable** (reconstructed from modes), not the Rhie–Chow flux.

**Pressure (Poisson form of continuity).** Writing the momentum matrix as
$\mathbf A_U=\operatorname{diag}(\mathbf a_U)+\text{off-diagonal}$, with $\mathbf r_{AU}=1/\mathbf a_U$,
$\mathbf H(U)=\mathbf b_U-(\text{off-diag})\,U$:
$$
\mathbf{HbyA}=\mathbf r_{AU}\,\mathbf H(U),\qquad
\varphi_{HbyA}=(\mathbf{HbyA})_f\!\cdot\mathbf S_f+(\mathbf r_{AU})_f\,\mathrm{ddtCorr}(U,\varphi),
$$
$$
\mathbf R_p(\mathbf q^n)=\big[\nabla\!\cdot(\mathbf r_{AU}\nabla p)\big]_h-\big[\nabla\!\cdot\varphi_{HbyA}\big]_h ,
$$
with the reference cell fixed by `setReference`. $\mathbf R_p=-\nabla\!\cdot\varphi_{RC}$ is the divergence of the
Rhie–Chow flux $\varphi_{RC}=\varphi_{HbyA}-(\mathbf r_{AU})_f\,(\nabla p)_f\cdot\mathbf S_f$ of the current
$(U,p)$.

**Turbulence.** $\mathbf R_k,\mathbf R_\omega$ are the SST transport equations assembled with the current
$U,\varphi,k,\omega$.

**Optional flux block** (`fluxResidual true`): $\mathbf R_\varphi=\varphi-\varphi_{RC}$ on internal faces.

The time-discrete problem at step $n$ is
$$
\mathbf R^n(\mathbf q^n)\equiv\mathbf R\big(\mathbf q^n;\mathbf q^{n-1},\mathbf q^{n-2},\boldsymbol\mu\big)
=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf R_U\\ \mathbf R_p\\ \mathbf R_k\\ \mathbf R_\omega\end{bmatrix}=\mathbf 0 .
$$

Both ROMs minimise a weighted norm of exactly this residual. **They differ only in the trial manifold**
in which $\mathbf q^n$ is sought (Sections 2–6). The weighting (Section 7) and the solver (Section 8) are the same.

## 2. Snapshots and preprocessing (common to both)

### 2.1 Centring (`snapshotCollectionMethod 1`)

Run $r$ stores the states at $t_j=j\,\Delta t_w$, $j=1,\dots,m_r$ ($\Delta t_w$ = `writeEvery`). The reference
of each run is its **first stored** state:
$$
\mathbf q^f_{0,r}=\mathbf q^f(t_1;\boldsymbol\mu_r)\qquad(t_1=\Delta t_w,\ \text{not the initial condition}),
$$
$$
\mathbf s^f_{r,j}=\mathbf q^f(t_j;\boldsymbol\mu_r)-\mathbf q^f_{0,r},\qquad f\in\mathcal F\cup\{\varphi\}.
$$
(The first centred snapshot of every run is therefore $\mathbf 0$.)

### 2.2 Per-run normalisation (`snapshotNormalization run`)

Only the copies used to *compute the modes* are normalised. The ROM coordinates are always computed from the
unnormalised $\mathbf s$.
$$
\sigma^f_r=\Big(\frac1{m_r}\sum_{j=1}^{m_r}\lVert\mathbf s^f_{r,j}\rVert_{\mathbf M}^2\Big)^{1/2},\qquad
\tilde{\mathbf s}^f_{r,j}=\mathbf s^f_{r,j}/\sigma^{g(f)}_r ,
$$
and the scale owner $g(f)$ is the only difference between the two approaches here:

| | segregated | stacked |
|---|---|---|
| $f\in\mathcal G$ | $g(f)=f$ | $g(f)=U$ |
| $f=\varphi$ | $g=U$ | $g=U$ |
| $f\notin\mathcal G$ | $g(f)=f$ | $g(f)=f$ |

In the stacked case a whole stacked snapshot $(\mathbf s^U,\mathbf s^p,\dots)_{r,j}$ is divided by **one** number.
Different numbers per field would change the ratio $p/U$ from run to run ($p\sim U^2$), and no shared coefficient
could then reproduce the unnormalised pairs. This is the same reason $\varphi$ always uses $\sigma^U_r$.

Collect $\tilde{\mathbf S}^f=[\tilde{\mathbf s}^f_{1},\dots,\tilde{\mathbf s}^f_{m}]\in\mathbb R^{n_f\times m}$
(all runs, in order).

## 3. Segregated POD

### 3.1 Method of snapshots, one eigenproblem per field

$$
\mathbf C^f=\tilde{\mathbf S}^{f\top}\mathbf M\,\tilde{\mathbf S}^f\in\mathbb R^{m\times m},\qquad
\mathbf C^f\mathbf W^f=\mathbf W^f\boldsymbol\Lambda^f,\quad \lambda^f_1\ge\lambda^f_2\ge\dots\ge0,
$$
$$
\boldsymbol\Phi^f=\tilde{\mathbf S}^f\,\mathbf W^f_{:,1:N_f}\,\big(\boldsymbol\Lambda^f_{1:N_f}\big)^{-1/2},
\qquad \boldsymbol\Phi^{f\top}\mathbf M\boldsymbol\Phi^f=\mathbf I_{N_f}.
$$
(ITHACA builds $\tilde{\mathbf S}^f\mathbf W^f$ and normalises each column in $\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\mathbf M}$, which is the
same thing.)

### 3.2 Optimality

By Eckart–Young in the $\mathbf M$-inner product, $\boldsymbol\Phi^f$ solves, **independently for each field**,
$$
\boldsymbol\Phi^f=\arg\min_{\boldsymbol\Psi^\top\mathbf M\boldsymbol\Psi=\mathbf I_{N_f}}
\sum_{n=1}^m\big\lVert\tilde{\mathbf s}^f_n-\boldsymbol\Psi\boldsymbol\Psi^\top\mathbf M\tilde{\mathbf s}^f_n\big\rVert_{\mathbf M}^2
=\sum_{i>N_f}\lambda^f_i ,
$$
and the captured energy is $\sum_{i\le N_f}\lambda^f_i/\operatorname{tr}\mathbf C^f$.

### 3.3 The flux modes (`getPhiModes`)

$\varphi$ is not POD'd. Its modes reuse the **temporal** coefficients of the velocity POD. With
$c_{ni}=\langle\boldsymbol\Phi^U_i,\tilde{\mathbf s}^U_n\rangle_{\mathbf M}=\sqrt{\lambda^U_i}\,W^U_{ni}$,
$$
\boldsymbol\Phi^\varphi_i=\sum_n\frac{c_{ni}}{\sum_l c_{li}^2}\,\tilde{\mathbf s}^\varphi_n
=\tilde{\mathbf S}^\varphi\,\mathbf W^U_{:,i}\,(\lambda^U_i)^{-1/2}.
$$
This is already a stacked POD of $(U,\varphi)$ in which $\varphi$ has **zero weight**: it does not influence
$\mathbf W^U$, it only follows it. Because of this, the face flux and the velocity always share the same coordinates.

## 4. Stacked POD (`computeJointPOD`)

### 4.1 Weights: why they are needed and how they are chosen

**Why a weight is unavoidable.** A stacked POD needs one inner product on the stacked state
$(\mathbf q^U,\mathbf q^p,\dots)$. The obvious candidate,
$\lVert\mathbf s^U\rVert_{\mathbf M}^2+\lVert\mathbf s^p\rVert_{\mathbf M}^2$, adds $(\mathrm{m/s})^2$ to
$(\mathrm{m^2/s^2})^2$, so it has no meaning. More concretely, the result depends on the units: expressing $p$ in
other units, $p\to\alpha p$, turns the correlation matrix into $\mathbf C^U+\alpha^2\mathbf C^p$, whose eigenvectors
change with $\alpha$. Any stacked POD therefore contains a choice of relative weights, explicit or hidden. Two things
that look like they could avoid it do not:

* **Non-dimensional variables** fix the weights through the reference scales ($w_f=1/q_{\text{ref},f}^2$). The scales
  are not unique ($\rho U^2$ or $\tfrac12\rho U^2$ changes $w_p$ by 4), and they do not equalise fluctuation
  variances. The tutorial is already non-dimensional ($U_{in}=1$, $D=1$, kinematic $p$), yet the pressure fluctuations
  carry about $1/27$ of the velocity's energy. aero-F works this way, with an unweighted Euclidean SVD of the
  non-dimensional conservative variables.
* **Run normalisation** (2.2) rescales *whole stacked snapshots*, one number per run for all fields of the group. It
  balances runs against each other, never fields against each other.

The weights are the missing per-field, run-independent scale. Dividing field $f$ by a constant $c_f$ in every run is
exactly the weight $w_f=1/c_f^2$. The code applies it as a weight, so the modes keep their physical units and the ROM
can reuse the same inner product (Section 6).

**How the weights are chosen in the code** (`computeJointPOD`), for each field $f\in\mathcal G$:

1. Take the jointly run-normalised snapshots $\tilde{\mathbf s}^f_n=\mathbf s^f_n/\sigma^U_{r(n)}$ (2.2).
2. Compute the field's **mean snapshot energy**
   $$
   \mathcal E_f=\frac1m\sum_{n=1}^m\big\lVert\tilde{\mathbf s}^f_n\big\rVert_{\mathbf M}^2=\frac1m\operatorname{tr}\mathbf C^f .
   $$
3. Set
   $$
   w_f=\frac{\varpi_f}{\mathcal E_f},
   $$
   where $\varpi_f$ is the user multiplier (`jointPODWeights`, default 1).

After weighting, the mean energy of field $f$ is $w_f\,\mathcal E_f=\varpi_f$. Hence:

* with the default $\varpi_f=1$, **every field of the group has the same mean energy**, and each contributes the share
  $1/|\mathcal G|$ of $\operatorname{tr}\mathbf C$;
* in general, field $f$ contributes the share $\varpi_f/\sum_{g\in\mathcal G}\varpi_g$. So $\varpi$ states directly
  how much each field should influence the shared temporal coefficients $\mathbf W$;
* $w_f$ has units $1/[f]^2$, so every term $w_f\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\mathbf M}^2$ is dimensionless and the result does not
  depend on the units.

**Tutorial values.** Run normalisation by $\sigma^U_r$ makes $\mathcal E_U=\frac1m\sum_r\sum_j\lVert\mathbf s^U_{r,j}\rVert_{\mathbf M}^2/(\sigma^U_r)^2=\frac1m\sum_r m_r=1$
exactly. The pressure has $\mathcal E_p=1/27.17$, so the log prints $(w_U,w_p)=(1,\,27.17)$.

**Choosing $\varpi$.**
* $\varpi=(1,1)$: neutral default, with $U$ and $p$ equally influential.
* $\varpi_p>1$: the leading modes describe the pressure better (e.g. when the forces are the target).
* $\varpi_p<1$: the velocity dominates. $\varpi_p=1/27.17$ reproduces the unweighted (aero-F-like) choice,
  $w_U=w_p=1$.

**Consistency with the residual weighting.** With an ideal preconditioner, LSPG would return the best approximation of
the FOM state in the norm set by the field scales of Section 7, $\sum_f\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\mathbf M}^2/\hat\sigma_f^2$
(Lindsay et al. 2022). The POD basis is optimal in $\sum_f w_f\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\mathbf M}^2$. The two agree when
$w_f\propto1/\hat\sigma_f^2$. In the tutorial $w_p/w_U=27.17$ and $(\hat\sigma_U/\hat\sigma_p)^2=(28.9/5.53)^2=27.3$, so
the default is consistent. If `preconditionerScales` is set by hand, `jointPODWeights` should be changed to match.

**Weights only matter through truncation.** If all modes are kept ($N=\operatorname{rank}$), the stacked subspace is the
range of the stacked snapshot matrix for any positive weights, and LSPG does not depend on the parametrisation of a
subspace. The weights decide *which* $N$-dimensional subspace of the snapshots is retained.

### 4.2 One eigenproblem

$$
\mathbf C=\sum_{f\in\mathcal G}w_f\,\mathbf C^f
=\sum_{f\in\mathcal G}w_f\,\tilde{\mathbf S}^{f\top}\mathbf M\tilde{\mathbf S}^f,
\qquad \mathbf C\mathbf W=\mathbf W\boldsymbol\Lambda .
$$
Equivalently, it is the thin SVD of the scaled, stacked snapshot matrix
$$
\mathbf Y=\begin{bmatrix}\sqrt{w_U}\,\mathbf M^{1/2}\tilde{\mathbf S}^U\\ \sqrt{w_p}\,\mathbf M^{1/2}\tilde{\mathbf S}^p\\ \vdots\end{bmatrix}
=\mathbf L\,\boldsymbol\Lambda^{1/2}\mathbf W^\top ,
$$
which is what He et al. (2021) do on the raw stacked state.

Modes are kept while $\lambda_i>10^{-12}\lambda_1$ (and up to `NmodesUout`), and the ROM uses the first
$N$ = `NmodesUproj`.

### 4.3 Modes of every field from the same temporal coefficients

$$
\hat{\boldsymbol\Phi}^f=\tilde{\mathbf S}^f\,\mathbf W_{:,1:N}\,\boldsymbol\Lambda_{1:N}^{-1/2},
\qquad f\in\mathcal G\cup\{\varphi\}.
$$
The stacked mode $i$ is the tuple $(\hat{\boldsymbol\Phi}^U_i,\hat{\boldsymbol\Phi}^p_i,\hat{\boldsymbol\Phi}^\varphi_i,\dots)$.

**Orthonormality holds only in the joint inner product:**
$$
\sum_{f\in\mathcal G}w_f\,\hat{\boldsymbol\Phi}^{f\top}\mathbf M\hat{\boldsymbol\Phi}^f
=\boldsymbol\Lambda^{-1/2}\mathbf W^\top\mathbf C\,\mathbf W\boldsymbol\Lambda^{-1/2}=\mathbf I_N,
\qquad\text{but}\qquad
\hat{\boldsymbol\Phi}^{f\top}\mathbf M\hat{\boldsymbol\Phi}^f\neq\mathbf I_N .
$$
Any per-field projection (e.g. `getCoeffs(U, Umodes)` alone) therefore gives coordinates that are **not**
the stacked ones (Section 6).

### 4.4 Optimality

$$
\big(\hat{\boldsymbol\Phi}^f\big)_{f\in\mathcal G}=\arg\min\ \sum_{n=1}^m\sum_{f\in\mathcal G}w_f
\big\lVert\tilde{\mathbf s}^f_n-\boldsymbol\Psi^f\mathbf c_n\big\rVert_{\mathbf M}^2
\quad\text{over bases }\boldsymbol\Psi^f\text{ and \textbf{shared} coefficients }\mathbf c_n\in\mathbb R^N,
$$
with minimum $\sum_{i>N}\lambda_i$. The constraint that $\mathbf c_n$ is shared means that, at equal $N$,
**each field's own projection error is at least its segregated one**:
$$
\sum_n\min_{\mathbf c}\lVert\tilde{\mathbf s}^f_n-\hat{\boldsymbol\Phi}^f\mathbf c\rVert_{\mathbf M}^2\ \ge\ \sum_{i>N}\lambda^f_i .
$$
(Tutorial, $N=15$: pressure projection error 2.0% segregated vs. a slightly larger value with the stacked basis.)

### 4.5 Limits of the weights

* $\varpi_p\to0$: $\mathbf W\to\mathbf W^U$, $\hat{\boldsymbol\Phi}^U\to\boldsymbol\Phi^U$ and
  $\hat{\boldsymbol\Phi}^p=\tilde{\mathbf S}^p\mathbf W^U(\boldsymbol\Lambda^U)^{-1/2}$. The pressure is *slaved*
  to the velocity POD exactly like $\varphi$ (3.3).
* $\varpi_p\to\infty$: the reverse, with the velocity slaved to the pressure POD.
* $\varpi_p=1$: a compromise in which both fields choose $\mathbf W$.

## 5. Trial manifold

### 5.1 Affine reference

For a new parameter, the references of the runs are blended by inverse-distance weighting:
$$
\bar{\mathbf q}^f(\boldsymbol\mu)=\sum_{r=1}^R\varpi_r(\boldsymbol\mu)\,\mathbf q^f_{0,r},\qquad
\varpi_r(\boldsymbol\mu)=\frac{(\lVert\boldsymbol\mu-\boldsymbol\mu_r\rVert+10^{-10})^{-1}}{\sum_s(\lVert\boldsymbol\mu-\boldsymbol\mu_s\rVert+10^{-10})^{-1}} .
$$

### 5.2 Linear part

Collect all field coordinates in the **full coordinate vector**
$$
\mathbf x=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf a^U\\ \mathbf a^p\\ \mathbf a^k\\ \mathbf a^\omega\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb R^{N_x},
\qquad N_x=N_U+N_p+N_k+N_\omega ,
$$
and the block reconstruction operator ($\varphi$ rides on the $U$ block)
$$
\mathbf Q=\operatorname{blkdiag}\!\Big(\begin{bmatrix}\boldsymbol\Phi^U\\ \boldsymbol\Phi^\varphi\end{bmatrix},\
\boldsymbol\Phi^p,\ \boldsymbol\Phi^k,\ \boldsymbol\Phi^\omega\Big).
$$

**Segregated:** $\mathbf x$ is free, and the reduced coordinates are $\boldsymbol\xi=\mathbf x$, $N_\xi=N_x$.

**Stacked** ($\mathcal G=\{U,p\}$): the coordinates are
$\boldsymbol\xi=[\hat{\mathbf a};\mathbf a^k;\mathbf a^\omega]\in\mathbb R^{N_\xi}$, $N_\xi=N+N_k+N_\omega$,
and they enter through a 0/1 **expansion matrix** $\mathbf E$ (`stateMap_`), with $\mathbf Q$ built from the
stacked bases $\hat{\boldsymbol\Phi}$:
$$
\mathbf x=\mathbf E\boldsymbol\xi,\qquad
\mathbf E=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf I_N&\mathbf 0&\mathbf 0\\ \mathbf I_N&\mathbf 0&\mathbf 0\\ \mathbf 0&\mathbf I_{N_k}&\mathbf 0\\ \mathbf 0&\mathbf 0&\mathbf I_{N_\omega}\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
\mathbf Q\mathbf E=\begin{bmatrix}\hat{\boldsymbol\Phi}^U&\mathbf 0&\mathbf 0\\ \hat{\boldsymbol\Phi}^\varphi&\mathbf 0&\mathbf 0\\ \hat{\boldsymbol\Phi}^p&\mathbf 0&\mathbf 0\\ \mathbf 0&\boldsymbol\Phi^k&\mathbf 0\\ \mathbf 0&\mathbf 0&\boldsymbol\Phi^\omega\end{bmatrix}.
$$
The segregated basis is block-diagonal in $(U,p)$. The stacked one is a **full column** in the $(U,\varphi,p)$
rows. For $\mathcal G=\mathcal F$, $\mathbf E=[\mathbf I_N;\mathbf I_N;\mathbf I_N;\mathbf I_N]$ and $N_\xi=N$.

### 5.3 Nonlinear completion

$$
\mathbf q(\boldsymbol\xi;\boldsymbol\mu)=\mathcal B\big(\bar{\mathbf q}(\boldsymbol\mu)+\mathbf Q\mathbf E\boldsymbol\xi\big),
$$
where $\mathcal B$:
1. imposes the inlet velocity $\boldsymbol\mu$ on the inlet patch and sets the inlet flux
   $\varphi_b=U_b\cdot\mathbf S_b$;
2. corrects the pressure boundary values;
3. floors $k$ and $\omega$ with the smooth bound
   $$
   b_\mu(x)=\mu_f+\mu_f\,\operatorname{softplus}\!\Big(\frac{x-\mu_f}{\mu_f}\Big),\qquad
   \mu_f=\beta\min_{\text{training}}^{+}f,\quad\beta=\texttt{turbulenceBoundFactor},
   $$
   which is $C^\infty$, so the finite-difference Jacobian does not see jumps.

$\mathcal B$ is identical in the two approaches.

### 5.4 What the stacked manifold enforces

If $\hat{\boldsymbol\Phi}^U$ has full column rank, then on the stacked manifold
$\hat{\mathbf a}=\big(\hat{\boldsymbol\Phi}^U\big)^{+}_{\mathbf M}(\mathbf q^U-\bar{\mathbf q}^U)$, with
$\mathbf X^{+}_{\mathbf M}=(\mathbf X^\top\mathbf M\mathbf X)^{-1}\mathbf X^\top\mathbf M$, and hence
$$
\boxed{\ \mathbf q^p-\bar{\mathbf q}^p=\mathbf L\,(\mathbf q^U-\bar{\mathbf q}^U),\qquad
\mathbf L=\hat{\boldsymbol\Phi}^p\big(\hat{\boldsymbol\Phi}^U\big)^{+}_{\mathbf M}\ }
$$
The pressure is a **fixed linear function of the velocity**, learned from the snapshots. The same holds for $\varphi$
in both approaches. In the segregated manifold, $\mathbf a^p$ is independent of $\mathbf a^U$.

Equivalently, the stacked manifold is the segregated layout (with bases $\hat{\boldsymbol\Phi}$) restricted by the
linear constraint
$$
(\mathbf I-\mathbf E\mathbf E^{+})\,\mathbf x=\mathbf 0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\mathbf a^U=\mathbf a^p .
$$

## 6. Projections: initial condition and coordinate scaling

### 6.1 What the projection is used for

The ROM needs the coordinates of given full fields in three places:

1. the **initial condition** of every online run (the fields at $t_0$);
2. the **coordinate scaling** $\mathbf D$ (6.5), from the coordinates of every training snapshot;
3. the **"projected" V1 consistency test**, which evaluates the ROM residual at the projected FOM trajectory.

In all three, the input is a set of deviations $\mathbf d^f=\mathbf q^f-\bar{\mathbf q}^f$, one per field, and the output
must be the coordinates of the point of the trial space **closest** to them.

### 6.2 Segregated: independent projections, one per field

Each field has its own coordinates and its own basis, which is orthonormal in $\mathbf M$. "Closest" separates field
by field into independent problems:
$$
\mathbf a^f=\arg\min_{\mathbf c}\lVert\mathbf d^f-\boldsymbol\Phi^f\mathbf c\rVert_{\mathbf M}
=\big(\boldsymbol\Phi^{f\top}\mathbf M\boldsymbol\Phi^f\big)^{-1}\boldsymbol\Phi^{f\top}\mathbf M\,\mathbf d^f
=\boldsymbol\Phi^{f\top}\mathbf M\,\mathbf d^f .
$$
Each one is just a set of inner products with the modes (`ITHACAutilities::getCoeffs` solves with the Gram matrix,
which is $\mathbf I$ up to round-off). Every field is fitted exactly as well as its basis allows.

### 6.3 Stacked: one coefficient vector for several fields, so a least-squares compromise

In the stacked case the **same** $\hat{\mathbf a}\in\mathbb R^N$ has to reproduce all fields of the group:
$$
\mathbf d^U\approx\hat{\boldsymbol\Phi}^U\hat{\mathbf a},\qquad \mathbf d^p\approx\hat{\boldsymbol\Phi}^p\hat{\mathbf a},\qquad\dots
$$
That is $\sum_{f\in\mathcal G}n_f$ equations for $N$ unknowns: an **overdetermined** system with, in general, no exact
solution. Fitting each field on its own would give
$$
\mathbf c^f=\big(\hat{\boldsymbol\Phi}^{f\top}\mathbf M\hat{\boldsymbol\Phi}^f\big)^{-1}\hat{\boldsymbol\Phi}^{f\top}\mathbf M\,\mathbf d^f ,\qquad f\in\mathcal G ,
$$
and $\mathbf c^U\neq\mathbf c^p$ as soon as $(\mathbf d^U,\mathbf d^p)$ is not exactly in the stacked span. That is always
the case for a new state, and also for the training snapshots once modes are truncated. One vector has to be chosen,
and the natural choice is the closest point **in the same weighted norm the POD was built in**:
$$
\hat{\mathbf a}=\arg\min_{\mathbf c\in\mathbb R^N}\ \sum_{f\in\mathcal G}w_f\,\big\lVert\mathbf d^f-\hat{\boldsymbol\Phi}^f\mathbf c\big\rVert_{\mathbf M}^2 .
$$
Setting the gradient to zero gives the normal equations
$$
\mathbf G\,\hat{\mathbf a}=\mathbf b,\qquad
\mathbf G=\sum_{f\in\mathcal G}\underbrace{w_f\,\hat{\boldsymbol\Phi}^{f\top}\mathbf M\hat{\boldsymbol\Phi}^f}_{\mathbf G_f},\qquad
\mathbf b=\sum_{f\in\mathcal G}w_f\,\hat{\boldsymbol\Phi}^{f\top}\mathbf M\,\mathbf d^f ,
$$
which is what `jointCoefficients` assembles and solves (with an $\mathbf L\mathbf D\mathbf L^\top$ factorisation).

**The solution is a weighted average of the per-field fits.** Since $\mathbf b=\sum_f\mathbf G_f\mathbf c^f$,
$$
\boxed{\ \hat{\mathbf a}=\Big(\sum_{f\in\mathcal G}\mathbf G_f\Big)^{-1}\sum_{f\in\mathcal G}\mathbf G_f\,\mathbf c^f\ }
$$
For a single mode ($N=1$), with $\gamma_f=w_f\lVert\hat{\boldsymbol\Phi}^f\rVert_{\mathbf M}^2$ the share of field $f$ in the
mode's weighted norm:
$$
\hat a=\frac{\gamma_U\,c^U+\gamma_p\,c^p}{\gamma_U+\gamma_p},\qquad
c^f=\frac{\langle\hat{\boldsymbol\Phi}^f,\mathbf d^f\rangle_{\mathbf M}}{\lVert\hat{\boldsymbol\Phi}^f\rVert_{\mathbf M}^2}.
$$
Each field "votes" with the weight it has in the mode. A mode that is mostly velocity is set mostly by the velocity, and
vice versa.

**Picture.** Figure 1 shows the smallest possible case: one stacked mode, and $U$ and $p$ reduced to one number each, so
the stacked trial space is a line through the origin with direction $(\hat\Phi^U,\hat\Phi^p)=(1,\,0.6)$. The deviation to
project, $(d^U,d^p)=(0.8,\,0.9)$, is not on the line.

* Fitting $U$ alone means moving vertically onto the line (orange, $c^U=0.80$). The velocity is reproduced exactly; the
  pressure is whatever the mode implies, $0.48$ instead of $0.9$.
* Fitting $p$ alone means moving horizontally (purple, $c^p=1.50$). The pressure is exact; the velocity overshoots, $1.5$
  instead of $0.8$.
* The weighted least-squares point (blue, $\hat a=0.99$) is the **orthogonal** projection in the weighted coordinates (left
  panel, $w_U=w_p=1$, equal axes). It always lies between the two per-field fits.
* The right panel shows where it lands for other weights: $w_p\to0$ slides it to the velocity fit, $w_p\to\infty$ to the
  pressure fit. This is how the weights of 4.1 enter the projection.

**Why not project a single field (e.g. only $U$)?** It is cheaper, but:
* it ignores the pressure data entirely ($\hat{\mathbf a}=\mathbf c^U$, and the pressure becomes whatever the velocity
  implies, $\hat{\boldsymbol\Phi}^p\mathbf c^U$);
* $\hat{\boldsymbol\Phi}^{f\top}\mathbf M\hat{\boldsymbol\Phi}^f$ alone is not $\mathbf I$ and can be badly conditioned. A
  stacked mode can have a tiny $U$ part (a mode carried by pressure), and $\mathbf c^U$ would then amplify noise along it;
* the result would depend on which field was picked, not on the basis.

**Why the POD weights, and not others?** With $w_f$ the stacked modes are orthonormal, $\mathbf G=\mathbf I_N$, so
$$
\hat a_i=\sum_{f\in\mathcal G}w_f\,\langle\hat{\boldsymbol\Phi}^f_i,\mathbf d^f\rangle_{\mathbf M},
$$
and the projection is orthogonal, with the Pythagorean split
$\sum_f w_f\lVert\mathbf d^f\rVert_{\mathbf M}^2=\lVert\hat{\mathbf a}\rVert^2+\sum_f w_f\lVert\mathbf d^f-\hat{\boldsymbol\Phi}^f\hat{\mathbf a}\rVert_{\mathbf M}^2$.
For the training snapshots this gives back exactly the POD coefficients: for the normalised snapshot
$\tilde{\mathbf s}_n$, $\hat{\mathbf a}_n=\boldsymbol\Lambda^{-1/2}\mathbf W^\top\mathbf C\,\mathbf e_n=\boldsymbol\Lambda^{1/2}\mathbf W_{n,:}^\top$
(for the unnormalised $\mathbf s_n$, multiply by $\sigma^U_{r(n)}$), and the residual sums to the truncation error
$\sum_{i>N}\lambda_i$. Other weights would give coordinates
that do not match the basis and would make the scaling $\mathbf D$ and the V1 "projected" state inconsistent with the POD.
The code still solves with $\mathbf G$ instead of assuming $\mathbf G=\mathbf I$, which is robust to round-off.

### 6.4 From full to reduced coordinates

Fields outside $\mathcal G$ are projected as in 6.2. The full coordinate vector $\mathbf x$ then holds $\hat{\mathbf a}$ in
the slot of every field of $\mathcal G$, and the reduced coordinates are recovered with the left inverse of $\mathbf E$:
$$
\boldsymbol\xi=(\mathbf E^\top\mathbf E)^{-1}\mathbf E^\top\mathbf x .
$$
$\mathbf E^\top\mathbf E$ is diagonal with entries $|\mathcal G|$ (shared coordinates) or 1 (others), so this averages the
copies of each shared coordinate. They are all equal here, so it simply extracts them.

### 6.5 Scaling of the coordinates

LM works with $\mathcal O(1)$ unknowns $\boldsymbol\eta$:
$$
\boldsymbol\xi=\mathbf D\boldsymbol\eta,\qquad \mathbf D=\operatorname{diag}(d_1,\dots,d_{N_\xi}),\qquad
d_j=\Big(\frac1m\sum_{n=1}^m\xi_{j,n}^2\Big)^{1/2},
$$
where $\xi_{j,n}$ are the coordinates of the **unnormalised** training snapshots $\mathbf s_n$, obtained with 6.2–6.3:
segregated per field, stacked with the joint projection. (If $d_j\le10^{-12}\max d$, $d_j$ is replaced by
$\max d$.)

The full coordinates seen by the residual are then
$$
\mathbf x(\boldsymbol\eta)=\mathbf E\,\mathbf D\,\boldsymbol\eta\qquad(\texttt{ReducedLSPG::fullState}),
$$
with $\mathbf E=\mathbf I$ in the segregated case.

## 7. Residual weighting (identical in both)

### 7.1 Why the residual must be weighted

For the FOM, any weighting gives the same answer: the root $\mathbf R=\mathbf 0$. The ROM cannot reach that root, because
the true solution is not in the trial space. LSPG instead returns the trial state with the **smallest weighted residual**,
and *which* state that is depends on the weights. The raw residual would be a poor norm to minimise, for three reasons:

* **Units differ between blocks.** The rows are volume-integrated balances: $\mathbf R_U$ in $\mathrm{m^4/s^2}$, $\mathbf R_p$ in
  $\mathrm{m^3/s}$, $\mathbf R_k$ in $\mathrm{m^5/s^3}$, and so on. Their sum of squares has no meaning, and the block with the
  largest numbers would decide the solution.
* **Cell sizes differ within a block.** A volume-integrated residual grows with $V_c$, so large far-field cells would
  dominate small near-wall cells.
* **Equations differ in stiffness.** The same residual value can mean a large or a small error in the unknown,
  depending on how strongly the equation reacts to that unknown.

The Jacobi weighting fixes all three at once by converting every residual row into **the change of the unknown it
implies**, in the unknown's own units. This is what the local Jacobi increment is (7.3).

### 7.2 The assembled equation of each block

At the current state, OpenFOAM assembles every equation of block $f$ as a sparse linear system in its own unknown,
linearised about the current fields (convection with the current $\varphi$, diffusion with the current $\nu_{\text{eff}}$, …):
$$
\mathbf A^f\,\mathbf q^f=\mathbf b^f,\qquad
\mathbf R^f=\mathbf A^f\mathbf q^f-\mathbf b^f .
$$
Row $c$ is the balance of cell $c$, integrated over its volume. Split the matrix into its diagonal and its neighbour part,
$$
\mathbf A^f=\mathbf D^f+\mathbf N^f,\qquad
D^f_{cc}=a^f_c\,V_c ,
$$
where $a^f_c$ is the diagonal **per unit volume**, i.e. what `fvMatrix::A()` returns. It includes the implicit boundary
contributions, and for $U$ it is shared by the three components. For example, in the momentum equation $a^U_c$ contains
$3/(2\Delta t)$ from BDF2, plus the convective and diffusive contributions of the faces of cell $c$.

### 7.3 One point-Jacobi sweep and the local Jacobi increment

**Point-Jacobi** is the simplest iterative solver for $\mathbf A\mathbf q=\mathbf b$. In one sweep, every cell solves *its own*
equation for *its own* value, with all neighbours frozen at their current values:
$$
q^{\text{new}}_c=\frac{1}{A_{cc}}\Big(b_c-\sum_{d\neq c}A_{cd}\,q_d\Big)\qquad\text{for every cell }c\text{ at once}.
$$
Subtracting $q_c$ and using $R_c=\sum_dA_{cd}q_d-b_c$ gives the change that the sweep applies:
$$
\boxed{\ \delta q_c=q^{\text{new}}_c-q_c=-\frac{R_c}{A_{cc}}=-\frac{R_c}{a_c\,V_c}\ }
\qquad\text{or, for all cells,}\qquad
\delta\mathbf q=-\mathbf D^{-1}\mathbf R .
$$
This $\delta q_c$ is the **local Jacobi increment**: how much the value of cell $c$ would have to change to satisfy the
balance of cell $c$ exactly, if its neighbours were correct. It has three useful properties:

* it is in the **units of the unknown**: $\mathrm{m/s}$ for $U$, $\mathrm{m^2/s^2}$ for $p$, and so on;
* it is **independent of the cell volume**: both $R_c$ and $A_{cc}$ scale with $V_c$;
* it is **zero exactly at a solution** of the FOM, so it measures the distance from the FOM fixed point cell by cell.

**Two cases that show what it measures.**

* *Time-derivative-dominated rows* (momentum, $k$, $\omega$, at moderate Courant number). There $A_{cc}\approx\tfrac{3}{2\Delta t}V_c$,
  so $\delta q_c\approx-\tfrac{2\Delta t}{3}\,R_c/V_c$: the correction the time step is missing. Such matrices are
  diagonally dominant, so the local increment is a good estimate of the actual error in the unknown.
* *The pressure equation* (a Laplacian, $A_{cc}=-\sum_{\text{faces}}(\mathbf r_{AU})_f|\mathbf S_f|/|\mathbf d_f|<0$, hence the
  $|\cdot|$). Here $\delta p_c$ is the pressure change that would cancel the continuity imbalance of cell $c$ with its
  neighbours fixed. A Laplacian is *not* diagonally dominant in that sense: an error that is smooth on the mesh (wavenumber
  $\kappa$, cell size $h$) produces a residual only $\sim(\kappa h)^2$ times its size. So $\delta p$ **sees cell-to-cell
  (checkerboard-like) pressure errors fully, and smooth pressure errors hardly at all.** This is the root of Section 9.

### 7.4 From increments to the LSPG norm

Two more factors turn the increments into the vector LM minimises. For row $c$ of block $f$:
$$
r^f_c=\frac{1}{\hat\sigma_f}\,\sqrt{V_c}\;\frac{R^f_c}{|a^f_c|\,V_c}
=\frac{\sqrt{V_c}}{\hat\sigma_f}\,\big|\delta q^f_c\big|\ \ (\text{up to the sign}),
\qquad
\hat\sigma_f=\Big(\frac1m\sum_n\frac{\lVert\mathbf s^f_n\rVert_{\mathbf M}^2}{|\Omega|}\Big)^{1/2}.
$$
* $\sqrt{V_c}$ makes the sum of squares an **integral over the domain**:
  $\sum_cV_c\,(\delta q_c)^2\approx\int_\Omega(\delta q)^2\,\mathrm d\Omega$. Refining the mesh adds cells but not weight.
* $\hat\sigma_f$, the RMS of the field's training fluctuations (`fieldScale_`, or `preconditionerScales`), makes the
  increment **relative to the typical fluctuation** of that field. This also makes it dimensionless, so the blocks can be
  added. Tutorial: $1/\hat\sigma=(5.53,\ 28.9,\ 277,\ 1.52)$ for $U,p,k,\omega$.

Altogether, the objective of every time step is
$$
\lVert\mathbf r\rVert_2^2=\sum_{f}\frac{1}{\hat\sigma_f^2}\,\big\lVert\delta\mathbf q^f\big\rVert_{\mathbf M}^2 ,
\qquad \delta\mathbf q^f=-(\mathbf D^f)^{-1}\mathbf R^f .
$$
In words: **how much one Jacobi sweep of the FOM would still change the ROM state, in $L^2$, relative to the typical
size of each field's fluctuations.** LSPG picks the trial state that the FOM would correct the least.

In matrix form,
$$
\mathbf r^n=\mathbf P^n\mathbf R^n,\qquad
\mathbf P^n=\hat{\boldsymbol\Sigma}^{-1}\,\mathbf M^{1/2}\,\big|\mathbf D^n\big|^{-1},
$$
with $\hat{\boldsymbol\Sigma}$ the diagonal of field scales. This is the "preconditioned LSPG" of Lindsay et al. (2022) with
the cheapest preconditioner, $\mathbf D^{-1}$ in place of $\mathbf A^{-1}$. With the exact $\mathbf A^{-1}$, $\delta\mathbf q$ would
be the full Newton correction, i.e. the actual error, and LSPG would return the best approximation of the FOM state in
the norm $\sum_f\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\mathbf M}^2/\hat\sigma_f^2$. Jacobi is close to that for the diagonally dominant blocks and
far from it for the pressure block, as shown above.

### 7.5 Frozen within a time step

$\mathbf D^n$ depends on the state (through $\varphi$, $\nu_{\text{eff}}$, …). It is computed **once per time step**, at the initial
guess of LM, and kept fixed during the iterations. If it were updated at every evaluation:
* the objective would change while LM minimises it, and the finite-difference Jacobian would mix in derivatives of the
  weights;
* LM could lower $\lVert\mathbf r\rVert$ by making the diagonals larger (smaller weights) instead of reducing the residual.

Freezing it makes each time step a fixed weighted least-squares problem.

### 7.6 The alternative: `residualPreconditioner none`

Rows are divided by $\sqrt{V_c}$ (removing the volume integration), and each block gets one weight, calibrated once from
the residual at the initial condition: $\omega_f=1/\max\big(\lVert\mathbf R^f_0\rVert,\ 10^{-4}\max_g\lVert\mathbf R^g_0\rVert\big)$,
so every block starts with norm 1 (`BlockResidual::calibrateWeights`). This is the analogue of aero-F's `BALANCED`
scaling. It balances blocks but does not account for how stiff each equation is, and the weights stay fixed for the whole
run.

## 8. Online solve

### 8.1 The LSPG problem at every time step

$$
\boldsymbol\eta^n=\arg\min_{\boldsymbol\eta\in\mathbb R^{N_\xi}}\ \tfrac12\big\lVert\mathbf r^n(\boldsymbol\eta)\big\rVert_2^2,
\qquad
\mathbf r^n(\boldsymbol\eta)=\mathbf P^n\,\mathbf R^n\Big(\mathcal B\big(\bar{\mathbf q}+\mathbf Q\mathbf E\mathbf D\boldsymbol\eta\big)\Big),
$$
with the old time levels $\mathbf q^{n-1},\mathbf q^{n-2}$ being the previous **ROM** states (stored by OpenFOAM's
`oldTime()` mechanism), so the ROM is a discrete-time LSPG (Carlberg et al. 2017).

### 8.2 Jacobian

By the chain rule, with $\mathbf R_{,f}=\partial\mathbf R/\partial\mathbf q^f$ and $\mathcal B'$ the (diagonal)
derivative of the completion:
$$
\mathbf J=\frac{\partial\mathbf r}{\partial\boldsymbol\eta}=\mathbf P\,\frac{\partial\mathbf R}{\partial\mathbf q}\,\mathcal B'\,\mathbf Q\,\mathbf E\,\mathbf D .
$$
It is never formed analytically. Each column is a central difference,
$$
\mathbf J_{:,j}\approx\frac{\mathbf r(\boldsymbol\eta+h_j\mathbf e_j)-\mathbf r(\boldsymbol\eta-h_j\mathbf e_j)}{2h_j},
\qquad h_j=\epsilon\max(|\eta_j|,1),\quad\epsilon=\texttt{jacobianStep}=10^{-5},
$$
which costs $2N_\xi$ residual assemblies per Jacobian.

**Column structure.** Dropping $\mathcal B'$ for readability:

*Segregated*
$$
\mathbf J=\Big[\ \underbrace{\mathbf P(\mathbf R_{,U}\boldsymbol\Phi^U+\mathbf R_{,\varphi}\boldsymbol\Phi^\varphi)\mathbf D_U}_{\mathbf J_U}\ \Big|\
\underbrace{\mathbf P\,\mathbf R_{,p}\boldsymbol\Phi^p\mathbf D_p}_{\mathbf J_p}\ \Big|\ \mathbf J_k\ \Big|\ \mathbf J_\omega\ \Big]
$$

*Stacked*
$$
\mathbf J=\Big[\ \underbrace{\mathbf P\big(\mathbf R_{,U}\hat{\boldsymbol\Phi}^U+\mathbf R_{,\varphi}\hat{\boldsymbol\Phi}^\varphi+\mathbf R_{,p}\hat{\boldsymbol\Phi}^p\big)\hat{\mathbf D}}_{\hat{\mathbf J}}\ \Big|\ \mathbf J_k\ \Big|\ \mathbf J_\omega\ \Big]
$$

In the stacked case **no column moves the pressure alone**. Every column is the sum of a velocity–flux
sensitivity and a pressure sensitivity, in the proportion seen in the training data.

### 8.3 Levenberg–Marquardt iteration

Eigen's MINPACK-based LM (`Eigen::LevenbergMarquardt`), initial guess $\boldsymbol\eta^{n-1}$:
$$
\big(\mathbf J^\top\mathbf J+\lambda\,\mathbf S^\top\mathbf S\big)\,\boldsymbol\delta=-\mathbf J^\top\mathbf r,
\qquad \boldsymbol\eta\leftarrow\boldsymbol\eta+\boldsymbol\delta\ \text{(trust-region acceptance)},
$$
with $\mathbf S$ MINPACK's adaptive column scaling. Settings: `ftol` $=10^{-6}$, `xtol` $=$ `gtol` $=0$, at most 10
`minimizeOneStep` calls per time step. After convergence
$\boldsymbol\xi^n=\mathbf D\boldsymbol\eta^n$ and $\mathbf x^n=\mathbf E\boldsymbol\xi^n$.

### 8.4 First-order optimality: what determines the pressure

At a minimiser, $\mathbf J^\top\mathbf r=\mathbf 0$.

*Segregated:* one set of equations per field block. In particular, $N_p$ equations act on the pressure alone:
$$
\mathbf D_p\boldsymbol\Phi^{p\top}\mathbf R_{,p}^\top\mathbf P^\top\mathbf r=\mathbf 0 .
$$
Since $\mathbf R_p$ and $\mathbf R_U$ are the only blocks that depend on $p$ (through $\nabla\!\cdot(\mathbf r_{AU}\nabla p)$ and
$\nabla p$), this says the weighted momentum and pressure-equation residuals are orthogonal to the pressure
sensitivities. The pressure is the minimiser of
$\lVert\mathbf P_U\mathbf R_U\rVert^2+\lVert\mathbf P_p\mathbf R_p\rVert^2$ **for the velocity found**.

*Stacked:* only the combined equations
$$
\hat{\mathbf D}\Big(\hat{\boldsymbol\Phi}^{U\top}\mathbf R_{,U}^\top+\hat{\boldsymbol\Phi}^{\varphi\top}\mathbf R_{,\varphi}^\top+\hat{\boldsymbol\Phi}^{p\top}\mathbf R_{,p}^\top\Big)\mathbf P^\top\mathbf r=\mathbf 0 .
$$
This is the Lagrangian stationarity condition of the segregated problem under the constraint of 5.4: with
$\mathbf J_{\mathbf x}=\partial\mathbf r/\partial\mathbf x$, $\ \mathbf E^\top\mathbf J_{\mathbf x}^\top\mathbf r=\mathbf 0$.

## 9. Why the pressure behaves differently (heuristic)

Consider a pressure perturbation $\delta p$ that is smooth on the mesh, with wavenumber $\kappa$ and cell size $h$
($\kappa h\ll1$), the kind of error that shifts the base pressure and the drag. Its effect on the weighted
residual:

* **Pressure block.** The Jacobi-scaled Laplacian damps smooth components:
  $$
  \lVert\mathbf P_p\,\mathbf R_{,p}\,\delta p\rVert\ \sim\ \frac{(\kappa h)^2}{\hat\sigma_p}\,\lVert\delta p\rVert_{\mathbf M}.
  $$
* **Momentum block.** $\delta p$ enters through $\nabla\delta p$, scaled by $1/a_U\le 2\Delta t/3$ (BDF2 diagonal):
  $$
  \lVert\mathbf P_U\,\mathbf R_{,p}^{(U)}\delta p\rVert\ \lesssim\ \frac{2\Delta t}{3}\,\frac{\kappa}{\hat\sigma_U}\,\lVert\delta p\rVert_{\mathbf M}.
  $$

Figure 2 plots both sensitivities for a 1-D mesh against the wavenumber of the error, together with what a *velocity*
change sees through the time derivative (the BDF2 diagonal, $\mathcal O(1)$ for any wavenumber):

![Sensitivity of the residual rows to an error of wavenumber kappa](images/doc/fig_sensitivity.png)

*Figure 2 – How strongly the weighted residual reacts to a unit error of wavenumber $\kappa$ (1-D model, Courant number
$\approx0.5$). Red: pressure error seen by the Jacobi-scaled pressure equation. Orange: pressure error seen by the
momentum equation through $\nabla p$. Blue dashed: velocity error seen by the momentum equation through $\partial_tU$.
Green band: the smooth errors that set the base pressure and the drag.*

In the green band the pressure equation sees a pressure error $10^2$–$10^4$ times more weakly than the momentum equation
sees a velocity error. (At $\kappa h=\pi$ the momentum curve drops to zero as well: that is the checkerboard mode, which
the Rhie–Chow term in the pressure equation is there to catch.)

Both are small. In the segregated Jacobian the pressure columns that represent smooth pressure changes therefore
have small norms, i.e. $\mathbf J$ has small singular values along them. Linearising around the solution, the
coordinate error satisfies roughly
$$
\lVert\delta\boldsymbol\eta\rVert\ \lesssim\ \frac{\lVert\mathbf r(\boldsymbol\eta_{\text{LSPG}})-\mathbf r(\boldsymbol\eta_{\text{proj}})\rVert}{\sigma_{\min}(\mathbf J)},
$$
so residual differences of $\mathcal O(1)$ turn into large smooth pressure errors. With more pressure modes there
are more such weak directions. Meanwhile the strongly weighted, *non-smooth* part of the pressure residual (truncation
noise amplified by the Laplacian) pulls the pressure coordinates away from the best approximation. The V1 test of the
segregated ROM shows this: the pressure block of the projected FOM state is $0.62$, against $0.22$ for the LSPG solution.

In the stacked Jacobian, each column $\hat{\mathbf J}_{:,i}$ also contains the velocity–flux part
$\mathbf P(\mathbf R_{,U}\hat{\boldsymbol\Phi}^U_i+\mathbf R_{,\varphi}\hat{\boldsymbol\Phi}^\varphi_i)$. Its norm is
bounded below by the time-derivative term:
$$
\lVert\mathbf P_U\mathbf R_{,U}\hat{\boldsymbol\Phi}^U_i\rVert\ \gtrsim\ \frac{\lVert\hat{\boldsymbol\Phi}^U_i\rVert_{\mathbf M}}{\hat\sigma_U}
$$
(on the ddt part, $\mathbf P_U\mathbf R_{,U}$ acts as $\tfrac{3/(2\Delta t)}{a_U}\,\mathbf I/\hat\sigma_U$, and the ratio
$\tfrac{3/(2\Delta t)}{a_U}\le1$ is of order one at Courant numbers $\lesssim1$, as here). A
smooth pressure change is no longer cheap, because it can only happen together with the velocity change that
accompanied it in the data. The pressure-equation residual is then *not* minimised separately: the stacked ROM
accepts a larger $\lVert\mathbf P_p\mathbf R_p\rVert$ (tutorial: 0.30 vs. 0.25) and gets a more accurate pressure.

The price is the rigidity of 5.4. If the true pressure is not $\mathbf L(\mathbf q^U-\bar{\mathbf q}^U)$, as can
happen away from the training parameters, the stacked ROM has no independent pressure coordinate to correct it.

## 10. A two-unknown model problem: why the stacked ROM gets the drag right

The argument of Section 9 can be made fully explicit on a problem small enough to solve by hand. It keeps the three
ingredients that matter: a stiff velocity equation, a pressure direction the residual barely sees, and a truncation
residual that cannot be driven to zero by the true solution.

### 10.1 Setup

**Unknowns.** One velocity coordinate $u$ and one pressure coordinate $p$, both scaled so that their training RMS is 1
(as $\boldsymbol\eta$ in 6.5). Think of $p$ as the coordinate of the smooth, symmetric pressure mode that sets the base
pressure, which is the drag-relevant one.

**Best approximation.** $(u^{\star},p^{\star})$ is the projection of the FOM solution onto the segregated trial space. Write the
errors as $e_u=u-u^{\star}$, $e_p=p-p^{\star}$.

**Weighted residual, linearised about the best approximation:**
$$
\mathbf r(e_u,e_p)=\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}\rho_U\\ \rho_p\end{bmatrix}}_{\mathbf r_0}
+\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}\alpha&\gamma\\ 0&\varepsilon\end{bmatrix}}_{\mathbf J}
\begin{bmatrix}e_u\\ e_p\end{bmatrix}.
$$
Row 1 is momentum, row 2 is the pressure equation, seen through the smooth pressure direction.

| Entry | Meaning | Size (Sections 7, 9) |
|---|---|---|
| $\alpha$ | momentum sensitivity to velocity, dominated by the BDF2 time derivative | $\mathcal O(1)$ |
| $\gamma$ | momentum sensitivity to the smooth pressure, through $\mathbf r_{AU}\nabla p$ | $\sim\tfrac{2\Delta t}{3}\kappa$, small |
| $\varepsilon$ | pressure-equation sensitivity to the smooth pressure (Jacobi-scaled Laplacian) | $\sim(\kappa h)^2$, very small |
| $\rho_U,\rho_p$ | residual of the best approximation (truncation), $\neq0$ | the V1 "projected" values |

The $(2,1)$ entry (pressure equation vs. velocity, through $\nabla\!\cdot\mathbf{HbyA}$) is set to 0 for readability.
With it, $\det\mathbf J=\alpha\varepsilon-\beta\gamma$ and nothing below changes qualitatively while $|\beta\gamma|\lesssim\alpha\varepsilon$.

**Output.** The drag is linear in the pressure: $C_d=C_d^{\star}+g\,e_p$, with $g$ the drag change per unit (training RMS) of
the mode. The velocity enters $C_d$ only through the viscous stress, which is negligible here (measured on the tutorial: viscous $C_d$ error below $0.004$).

**Training relation.** In the snapshots the two coordinates move together, $p\approx\ell\,u$. The stacked trial space is
the line $\{(a,\ell a)\}$, and the best approximation violates it by $\delta=p^{\star}-\ell u^{\star}$ (zero if the FOM state obeys the
training relation exactly).

### 10.2 Segregated LSPG

There are two unknowns and two equations, $\mathbf J$ is invertible, and LSPG drives the residual to **zero**:
$$
\begin{bmatrix}e_u\\ e_p\end{bmatrix}=-\mathbf J^{-1}\mathbf r_0
=\begin{bmatrix}-\rho_U/\alpha+\gamma\rho_p/(\alpha\varepsilon)\\[2pt] -\rho_p/\varepsilon\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
\boxed{\ \delta C_d^{\text{seg}}=-\,g\,\frac{\rho_p}{\varepsilon}\ }
$$
The truncation residual of the pressure equation, $\rho_p$, is cancelled by moving the pressure along its **weakest**
direction, and the move is amplified by $1/\varepsilon\sim(\kappa h)^{-2}$. The velocity barely notices: its error stays
of the order of $\rho_U/\alpha+\gamma\rho_p/(\alpha\varepsilon)$, small next to the pressure error because $\gamma\ll1$. A small residual does not mean a small
error. Here the residual is exactly zero, and the pressure error is the largest of all.

### 10.3 Stacked LSPG

Now $(e_u,e_p)=(e,\ \ell e-\delta)$, with $e=a-u^{\star}$ the only unknown:
$$
\mathbf r(e)=\mathbf r_0'+\mathbf j\,e,\qquad
\mathbf j=\mathbf J\begin{bmatrix}1\\ \ell\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\alpha+\gamma\ell\\ \varepsilon\ell\end{bmatrix},\qquad
\mathbf r_0'=\mathbf r_0-\mathbf J\begin{bmatrix}0\\ \delta\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\rho_U-\gamma\delta\\ \rho_p-\varepsilon\delta\end{bmatrix}.
$$
One unknown and two equations: LSPG is a 1-D least-squares problem,
$$
e=-\frac{\mathbf j^\top\mathbf r_0'}{\mathbf j^\top\mathbf j}
=-\frac{(\alpha+\gamma\ell)(\rho_U-\gamma\delta)+\varepsilon\ell(\rho_p-\varepsilon\delta)}{(\alpha+\gamma\ell)^2+\varepsilon^2\ell^2}.
$$
To leading order in $\varepsilon$:
$$
\boxed{\ e_p^{\text{stk}}=\ell e-\delta\ \approx\ -\frac{\ell\,\rho_U}{\alpha+\gamma\ell}\ -\ \frac{\alpha}{\alpha+\gamma\ell}\,\delta\ -\ \frac{\varepsilon\,\ell^2\rho_p}{(\alpha+\gamma\ell)^2}\ }
$$
The coordinate now moves along $\mathbf j$, which is dominated by the stiff momentum row ($\alpha$). The pressure-equation
residual $\rho_p$ enters only multiplied by $\varepsilon$, and is **left in the residual** instead of being cancelled by a
large pressure excursion: the final residual is $\approx(0,\ \rho_p)$. The pressure error is set by the velocity
truncation residual and by $\delta$, the violation of the training relation.

### 10.4 Numbers

Take values in the range of the tutorial: $\alpha=1$, $\gamma=0.05$, $\varepsilon=0.01$, $\ell=1$, $\rho_U=0.005$,
$\rho_p=0.002$, $g=0.3$, and $\delta=0$ (in-sample).

| | $e_u$ | $e_p$ | $\delta C_d$ | final $\lVert\mathbf r\rVert$ | p-equation residual |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Segregated | $+0.0050$ | $-0.200$ | $-0.060$ | $0$ | $0$ |
| Stacked | $-0.0048$ | $-0.0048$ | $-0.0014$ | $0.0020$ | $0.0020$ |

This reproduces what the tutorial shows (runs D and E):
* similar velocity errors in both ROMs;
* a pressure error, and a drag bias, about 40 times larger in the segregated ROM;
* the segregated ROM has the *smaller* pressure-equation residual (D: 0.25 vs. E: 0.30), because it buys it with a
  pressure excursion;
* a drag bias of order $-0.06$ for the segregated ROM, as in run D ($-0.07$ to $-0.09$).

### 10.5 The geometric picture: Jacobian directions

LSPG only ever moves the residual along the **columns of the Jacobian**: changing coordinate $j$ by $e_j$ moves the
residual by $e_j\,\mathbf J_{:,j}$. Everything above follows from what those columns look like.

**The columns.** With the numbers of 10.4,
$$
\mathbf J_u=\begin{bmatrix}\alpha\\0\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}1\\0\end{bmatrix},\qquad
\mathbf J_p=\begin{bmatrix}\gamma\\\varepsilon\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}0.05\\0.01\end{bmatrix},\qquad
\mathbf j_{\text{stacked}}=\mathbf J_u+\ell\,\mathbf J_p=\begin{bmatrix}1.05\\0.01\end{bmatrix}.
$$
* $\mathbf J_u$ is long: a velocity change is seen strongly, through the time derivative.
* $\mathbf J_p$ is **short** ($\lVert\mathbf J_p\rVert=0.051$) and only $11^\circ$ away from $\mathbf J_u$. A smooth
  pressure change is seen weakly, and mostly by the momentum row.
* The two columns are nearly parallel, so $\mathbf J$ is ill-conditioned: singular values $1.001$ and $0.010$, a
  condition number of 100. The small one belongs to the right singular vector
  $\mathbf v_2\approx(-0.05,\ 1)$: **almost pure pressure**.

**Residual space (Figure 3).** The best approximation leaves the residual $\mathbf r_0=(5,\,2)\cdot10^{-3}$ (black
square). The task of each ROM is to move it as close to $\mathbf r=\mathbf 0$ as its columns allow.

* *Segregated* (red). With two independent columns it can reach $\mathbf 0$ exactly. The vertical part of $\mathbf r_0$,
  $\rho_p=0.002$, can only be removed by $\mathbf J_p$, whose vertical component is $\varepsilon=0.01$: this needs
  $e_p=-\rho_p/\varepsilon=-0.2$. That large step also moves the residual horizontally by $-0.2\gamma=-0.01$, so the
  velocity has to compensate with $e_u=+0.005$. The residual reaches zero, and the price is a pressure error 40 times
  the velocity error.
* *Stacked* (blue). There is one column, $\mathbf j$, which is almost horizontal. The reachable residuals are the line
  $\mathbf r_0+t\,\mathbf j$, and LSPG takes the point of that line closest to the origin. It removes the horizontal
  (momentum) part and keeps the vertical part: the final residual is $\approx(0,\ \rho_p)$. Nothing forces a large pressure
  move, because there is no column that can make one on its own.

![Residual space](images/doc/fig_residual_space.png)

*Figure 3 – Residual space $(r_1,r_2)$ = (momentum, pressure equation). Red: the segregated LSPG solution reaches
$\mathbf r=\mathbf 0$ through a large step along the short column $\mathbf J_p$. Blue: the stacked LSPG solution is the
orthogonal projection of $\mathbf 0$ onto the reachable line $\mathbf r_0+t\,\mathbf j$.*

**Coordinate space (Figure 4).** The same problem, seen in the errors $(e_u,e_p)$. The grey lines are the level sets of
$\lVert\mathbf r\rVert$: very elongated ellipses (aspect ratio 100) along $\mathbf v_2$, i.e. a long valley in which the
residual hardly changes while the pressure changes a lot.

* The segregated solution is the **bottom of the valley** (red dot), wherever that is. Because the valley is flat along
  $e_p$, a tiny residual $\rho_p$ shifts the bottom far along it: to $e_p=-0.2$, outside the green band in which
  $|\delta C_d|<0.01$.
* The stacked solution must stay on the blue line $e_p=\ell e_u$ (the learned relation). It takes the point of the line
  with the smallest residual, i.e. where the line crosses the valley (blue dot). Since the line cuts the valley almost
  at right angles, that point is close to the best approximation, and inside the green band.

![Coordinate space](images/doc/fig_error_space.png)

*Figure 4 – Coordinate space $(e_u,e_p)$, measured from the best approximation (black square). Grey: level sets of the
weighted residual. Blue line: the stacked trial space. Green band: $|\delta C_d|<0.01$. Note the different axis ranges:
the valley is even more elongated than it looks.*

The same picture explains the observations of Sections 9 and 11 in words: **the segregated ROM finds the minimum
residual, but the minimum lies at the far end of a flat valley; the stacked ROM is not allowed to walk along the
valley.**

### 10.6 When the stacked ROM is better, and when it is not

Comparing the boxed results, the stacked ROM gives the smaller drag error roughly when
$$
\big|\delta\big|+\frac{\ell\,|\rho_U|}{\alpha}\ \lesssim\ \frac{|\rho_p|}{\varepsilon}.
$$
In words: **the learned pressure–velocity relation must be accurate to within the amplified pressure truncation error
of the segregated ROM.** In the example the right-hand side is 0.2, i.e. 20% of the mode's training RMS. In-sample,
$\delta$ is a truncation effect and is small. Away from the training parameters, $\delta$ grows with the error of the linear
map $\mathbf L$ (5.4), and the advantage shrinks or reverses.

Figure 5 shows both dependencies:

* **Left, versus $\varepsilon$.** The segregated drag error grows like $1/\varepsilon$: the smoother the pressure error
  (smaller $\kappa h$, hence smaller $\varepsilon$, Figure 2), the worse it gets. The stacked error is flat, set by the
  velocity truncation $\rho_U$. Only when the pressure equation sees the error well ($\varepsilon\gtrsim0.3$, i.e.
  non-smooth errors) is the segregated ROM as good.
* **Right, versus $\delta$.** The segregated error does not depend on $\delta$, since it has no learned relation to
  violate. The stacked error grows linearly with $\delta$, and the two cross at $\delta\approx0.21$. In-sample
  ($\delta$ small, green band) the stacked ROM is far better. The crossover is the condition above.

![Drag error](images/doc/fig_drag_error.png)

*Figure 5 – Drag error of the model problem. Left: versus the pressure-row sensitivity $\varepsilon$ (with $\delta=0$).
Right: versus the violation $\delta$ of the learned pressure–velocity relation (with $\varepsilon=0.01$).*

Two remarks:

* The stacked ROM puts the *whole* violation $\delta$ on the pressure ($e_p\approx-\delta$, $e_u\approx0$), not half on
  each field as the weighted projection of 6.3 would. The stiff momentum row keeps the velocity at its best value and the
  pressure takes up the inconsistency.
* With $N_p$ pressure modes the segregated error is $e_{p,i}=-\rho_{p,i}/\varepsilon_i$ for each weak direction $i$, and the
  drag error is $\sum_i g_i\rho_{p,i}/\varepsilon_i$. Every additional smooth pressure mode adds another amplified term.
  This matches the observed growth of the segregated pressure and drag errors with $N$ (Section 9, runs B→C→D). In the
  stacked ROM, additional modes come with their velocity part, and the amplification does not occur.

## 11. Summary

| | Segregated | Stacked ($\mathcal G=\{U,p\}$) |
|---|---|---|
| Run normalisation | $\sigma^f_r$ per field ($\varphi$: $\sigma^U_r$) | $\sigma^U_r$ for all of $\mathcal G\cup\{\varphi\}$ |
| Eigenproblem(s) | $\mathbf C^f\mathbf W^f=\mathbf W^f\boldsymbol\Lambda^f$, one per field | $\big(\sum_{\mathcal G}w_f\mathbf C^f\big)\mathbf W=\mathbf W\boldsymbol\Lambda$ |
| Modes | $\boldsymbol\Phi^f=\tilde{\mathbf S}^f\mathbf W^f(\boldsymbol\Lambda^f)^{-1/2}$ | $\hat{\boldsymbol\Phi}^f=\tilde{\mathbf S}^f\mathbf W\boldsymbol\Lambda^{-1/2}$ |
| Orthonormality | per field in $\mathbf M$ | joint, $\sum w_f\hat{\boldsymbol\Phi}^{f\top}\mathbf M\hat{\boldsymbol\Phi}^f=\mathbf I$ |
| Per-field projection error at equal $N$ | optimal | $\ge$ segregated |
| Coordinates | $[\mathbf a^U;\mathbf a^p;\mathbf a^k;\mathbf a^\omega]$, $N_\xi=\sum N_f$ | $[\hat{\mathbf a};\mathbf a^k;\mathbf a^\omega]$, $N_\xi=N+N_k+N_\omega$ |
| Basis matrix in $(U,\varphi,p)$ rows | block-diagonal | full column (shared) |
| $p$ vs. $U$ on the manifold | independent | $\mathbf q^p-\bar{\mathbf q}^p=\mathbf L(\mathbf q^U-\bar{\mathbf q}^U)$ |
| Projection (IC, scaling) | per field, `getCoeffs` | joint weighted LS, `jointCoefficients` |
| Pressure columns of $\mathbf J$ | separate, weak for smooth $\delta p$ | merged with velocity columns |
| Optimality conditions on $p$ | $N_p$ own equations | none of its own, combined with $U$ |
| Residual evaluations per Jacobian | $2\sum_f N_f$ (tutorial: 120) | $2(N+N_k+N_\omega)$ (tutorial: 90) |
| Residual weighting, LM, completion $\mathcal B$ | identical | identical |
